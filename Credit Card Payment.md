1. **Total Amount Due**
- The full bill amount for that billing cycle.
- Includes: all purchases + EMI amounts (if any) + fees + interest (if any).
- This is what you must pay by the due date to avoid interest.
- Paying this = clean slate, no interest (except on cash withdrawals in some cases).
1. **Minimum Amount Due (MAD)**
- Smallest amount the bank demands to keep your account “not defaulted.”
- Usually 5% of total due or a fixed amount (varies by bank).
- Paying only this avoids late fees and credit score damage.
- But the remaining amount starts attracting high interest (often 30–45% annually in India).
- This is the bank’s favorite number. Not yours.
1. **Current Outstanding**
- The total amount you owe right now at this moment.
- Includes new purchases made after the bill was generated.
- This number keeps changing daily.
- It may be higher than your “Total Amount Due” if you've spent more after the statement date.
Simple timeline view:
- Statement generated → “Total Amount Due” is fixed.
- After that → new spends increase “Current Outstanding.”
- Pay before due date → avoid interest (if you clear total due).
Think of it like this:
Minimum due keeps you legally safe.
Total due keeps you financially safe.
Ignoring both keeps banks very happy.
Credit cards are powerful tools — but mathematically, they’re designed to reward discipline and punish delay.
