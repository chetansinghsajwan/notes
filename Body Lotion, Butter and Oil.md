The primary differences between body lotion, body butter, and body oil lie in their water-to-oil ratios, texture, absorption rates, and ideal use cases.
|   |   |   |   |   |
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**Product Type**|**Water vs. Oil Ratio**|**Texture**|**Absorption Rate**|**Best Used For**|
|**Body Lotion**|High water, low oil|Lightweight, fluid|Fast (under 1 minute)|Daily hydration, normal to slightly dry skin, hot/humid weather.|
|**Body Oil**|100% oil, 0% water|Liquid, slick|Moderate (2 to 5 minutes)|Locking in existing moisture, adding a glowing sheen.|
|**Body Butter**|High oil/butter, low to 0% water|Thick, dense, creamy|Slow (5 to 10 minutes)|Intense nourishment, extremely dry patches (elbows, knees), winter care.|
**Body Lotion**
- **Formulation:** An emulsion of water and oil where water dominates.
- **Function:** Delivers immediate hydration to the skin because of its water content. It penetrates the surface quickly without leaving a heavy or greasy residue.
- **Application:** Ideal for daily use after a shower, especially during hot and humid months in tropical climates.
**Body Oil**
- **Formulation:** Pure concentrated oils (such as jojoba, almond, or coconut oil) without any water or fillers.
- **Function:** Acts as an occlusive barrier. It does not technically add moisture (water) to the skin; instead, it seals in the water that is already present.
- **Application:** Most effective when applied directly onto damp skin right after showering to trap the surface water.
**Body Butter**
- **Formulation:** Typically uses heavy plant-based fats like shea, cocoa, or mango butter mixed with carrier oils. Contains very little to no water.
- **Function:** Provides deep, long-lasting barrier protection. It creates a thick layer over the skin to prevent moisture loss over extended periods.
- **Application:** Best suited for severely dry skin, cracked heels, rough elbows, or for overnight repair during dry winter seasons.
