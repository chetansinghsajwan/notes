- **Fixed Deposit (FD)** is an agreement where you **deposit a principal amount** with a bank/NBFC for a **fixed tenure** at a **fixed interest rate**.
- During the tenure, the **interest rate does not change**.
- On maturity, you receive **principal + interest**.
**How it works**
- You choose:
	- **Principal** (lump sum amount)
	- **Tenure** (time period)
	- **Interest payout option**
- Bank uses your money for lending.
- In return, bank pays you **guaranteed interest**.
**Interest mechanics**
- Interest is usually **compound interest**.
- Compounding is commonly **quarterly**.
- Two payout types:
	- **Cumulative FD**: interest added back, paid at maturity.
	- **Non-cumulative FD**: interest paid monthly/quarterly/etc.
**Tenure**
- Ranges from **7 days to 10 years**.
- Longer tenure does **not always** mean higher interest.
- Rates depend on bank policy and RBI signals.
**Returns**
- Returns are **known in advance**.
- No link to stock market or NAV.
- Example:
	- Principal: ₹1,00,000
	- Rate: 7% p.a.
	- Tenure: 3 years
	- Maturity ≈ ₹1.22–1.23 lakh (compound)
**Safety**
- Bank FDs are insured up to **₹5,00,000 per depositor per bank**.
- Very low risk compared to mutual funds or stocks.
- NBFC FDs offer higher rates but **slightly higher risk**.
**Liquidity**
- Premature withdrawal allowed.
- **Penalty**: usually 0.5%–1% lower interest.
- Some FDs have **lock-in** (no early withdrawal).
**Taxation**
- FD interest is **fully taxable**.
- Taxed as **Income from Other Sources**.
- **TDS** deducted if interest crosses threshold.
- **5-year tax-saving FD**:
	- Eligible for **Section 80C** (up to ₹1.5 lakh).
	- Interest still taxable.
	- No premature withdrawal.
**Who should use FD**
- Capital safety is priority.
- Short to medium-term goals.
- Emergency funds (via FD laddering).
- Not ideal for long-term inflation beating.
**One-line summary**
- **FD = safe, time-bound investment with guaranteed returns and low risk.**
