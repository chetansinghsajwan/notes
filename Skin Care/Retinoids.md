Vitamin A–based compounds used in skincare and medicine.
- **What they do**:
	- Speed up skin cell turnover
	- Unclog pores
	- Increase collagen
	- Reduce acne, wrinkles, pigmentation
- **How they work (brief science)**:
    
	They bind to skin cell receptors → change gene activity → newer, healthier skin cells come up faster.
    
- **Main types (weak → strong)**:
	- **Retinol** – OTC, mild
	- **Retinal (retinaldehyde)** – OTC, stronger than retinol
	- **Tretinoin** – prescription
	- **Adapalene** – acne-focused
	- **Tazarotene** – strongest, prescription
- **Common side effects**:
	- Dryness
	- Peeling
	- Redness
	- Sun sensitivity
- **Basic usage rules**:
	- Use at **night**
	- Start **2–3 times/week**
	- Always use **sunscreen** in daytime
- **Who should avoid / be careful**:
	- Pregnant or trying to conceive
	- Very sensitive or damaged skin (without medical advice)
