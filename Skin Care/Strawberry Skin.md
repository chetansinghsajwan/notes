It is a **cosmetic skin appearance** where hair follicles look like dark dots due to **clogged or oxidized pores.**
It is most commonly seen on the legs, especially after shaving.
---
Sometimes also called as ==Keratosis Pilaris==.

> Note: Keratosis Pilaris is medical condition while Strawberry skin refers to the visual appearance of the skin. Which means keratosis pilaris can be one of the reasons for the strawberry skin appearance, but may not be the only.
---
==Causes of strawberry legs — sorted by likelihood (most → least common)==
1. **Clogged hair follicles (sebum + dead skin)**
	- Especially noticeable after shaving.
2. **Open pores + oxidation after shaving**
	- Very common in people who shave frequently.
3. **Dry skin (xerosis)**
	- Amplifies visibility of dark pores.
	- Extremely common contributor.
4. **Ingrown hairs (pseudofolliculitis)**
	- Common with curly/coarse hair.
5. **Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH)**
	- Happens after repeated irritation or ingrowns.
6. **Keratosis pilaris**
	- Less common on legs than arms.
	- Texture is usually rough.
7. **Folliculitis (bacterial or fungal)**
	- Least common cause.
	- Usually itchy, red, or painful (not just dark dots).
**Rule of thumb**
- If skin is **smooth + dotted** → top 3 causes.
- If **bumpy / itchy / painful** → lower-list causes.
==How to treat causes of strawberry legs?==
- **Follicular plugging (clogged pores)**
	- Salicylic acid 1–2% (2–3×/week)
	- Lactic / glycolic acid 5–10%
	- Daily moisturizer (urea, ceramides)
- **Open pores + oxidation after shaving**
	- Shave after warm shower
	- Sharp razor + shaving gel
	- Rinse with cool water
	- Moisturize immediately
- **Dry skin (xerosis cutis)**
	- Daily thick moisturizer
	- Ingredients: urea (10–20%), glycerin, ceramides
	- Avoid hot water showers
- **Ingrown hairs (pseudofolliculitis)**
	- Salicylic or glycolic acid
	- Avoid very close shaving
	- Switch to trimmer or laser hair removal
- **Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH)**
	- Niacinamide or azelaic acid
	- Gentle exfoliation
	- Sunscreen on exposed legs
	- Reduce shaving irritation
- **Keratosis pilaris**
	- Long-term use of urea + AHA/BHA
	- Consistency matters more than strength
	- Cannot cure, only control
- **Folliculitis**
	- Stop shaving temporarily
	- Antibacterial/antifungal wash
	- Dermatologist if painful or spreading
- **Best overall long-term option**
	- Laser hair removal → reduces hair, ingrowns, and clogged follicles
**Rule of thumb**
- Black dots → salicylic acid
- Dry + rough → urea + AHA
- Recurrent ingrowns → laser
==Salicylic acid vs glycolic acid for strawberry skin==
**Salicylic acid (BHA)**
- Oil-soluble → **goes deeper into pores**
- Best for **clogged follicles + ingrown hairs**
- Good for people with **oily or acne-prone skin**
- Typical strength: **1–2%**
- Less irritation for many
**Glycolic acid (AHA)**
- Water-soluble → **surface exfoliation**
- Good for **dry skin + overall texture**
- Helps fade **surface pigmentation**
- Typical strength: **5–10%**
- Can be more irritating if skin is sensitive
**Which to pick**
- **Black-dot / clogged pore focus:** _Salicylic acid wins_
- **Dry skin + overall smoothness:** _Glycolic acid helps_
- Combination can work well (use on different days)
