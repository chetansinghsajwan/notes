**Skin exfoliation** = removing **dead skin cells** from the surface of your skin.
==**Why is it needed?**==
- Skin **naturally sheds** dead cells
- Sometimes shedding is **incomplete or slow**
- Dead cells then **accumulate on the surface**
- Exfoliation removes this buildup
- Prevents **clogged pores** and **rough texture**
==**Types of exfoliation**==
- **Physical exfoliation**: removes dead cells using **friction**
- **Chemical exfoliation**: removes dead cells using **acids or enzymes**
## Physical Exfoliation
- Uses **mechanical friction** to remove dead skin cells
- Involves **scrubs, brushes, gloves, or tools**
- Works by **physically lifting and scraping** dead cells from the skin surface
  
**Pre-steps (before physical exfoliation)**
- Clean skin to remove dirt and oil
- Use **lukewarm water** to soften dead skin
- Ensure skin has **no cuts, burns, or irritation**
  
**During steps**
- Use **light pressure only**
- Move in **small circular motions**
- Limit time to **30–60 seconds (face)**, **1–2 minutes (body)**
- Avoid sensitive areas (eyes, broken skin)
  
**Post-steps (after exfoliation)**
- Rinse thoroughly with lukewarm water
- Pat dry (do not rub)
- Apply **moisturizer immediately**
- Apply **sunscreen** if skin is exposed
  
**Physical exfoliating materials (most effective → least effective at removing dead skin)**
- **Pumice / stone** – highest abrasion, removes thick dead skin fast
- **Crushed nut shells / apricot kernels** – very aggressive scraping
- **Salt (coarse)** – strong abrasion
- **Sugar** – effective but less consistent
- **Viscose (rayon) fiber** – strong fabric friction
- **Natural plant fibers / loofah** – moderate abrasion
- **Rice powder / oatmeal (fine)** – mild abrasive action
- **Jojoba beads** – controlled, moderate effectiveness
- **Microfiber cloth** – gentle, slower exfoliation
- **Silicone** – lowest abrasion, relies on repeated use
## Chemical Exfoliation
**What it is**
- Uses **acids or enzymes** to remove dead skin cells
- Breaks **chemical bonds** between skin cells
- Mimics and accelerates **natural skin shedding**
**Why it’s different from physical exfoliation**
- No friction or scraping
- More **uniform and controlled** exfoliation
- Reaches **inside pores**, not just surface
**Main types**
- **AHAs (Alpha Hydroxy Acids)**
	- Water-soluble
	- Work on skin surface
	- Best for **dry, dull, uneven skin**
	- Examples: glycolic, lactic, mandelic
	- Smaller molecules means higher penetration, thats why sensitive skins should use lactic acid which has higher molecular size.
	- better to use at night, since it leaves skin exposed.
- **BHAs (Beta Hydroxy Acids)**
	- Oil-soluble
	- Penetrate **inside pores**
	- Best for **oily, acne-prone skin**
	- Example: salicylic acid
- **PHAs (Poly Hydroxy Acids)**
	- Larger molecules, slower penetration
	- Very gentle
	- Best for **sensitive skin**
	- Examples: gluconolactone, lactobionic acid
- **Enzymatic exfoliants**
	- Use fruit enzymes
	- Break down dead cells only
	- Mildest form
	- Examples: papain (papaya), bromelain (pineapple)
**What it helps with**
- Dull skin
- Uneven texture
- Clogged pores
- Acne and blackheads
- Hyperpigmentation
- Fine lines (long-term use)
**How often**
- AHAs/BHAs: **1–3 times per week** (depends on strength)
- PHAs/enzymes: **2–4 times per week**
- Overuse damages skin barrier
**Risks**
- Irritation, redness, burning
- Increased **sun sensitivity**
- Over-exfoliation leads to acne and sensitivity
**Critical rules**
- Start **low concentration**
- Do **not mix** with other strong actives initially
- Always use **moisturizer**
- **Sunscreen is mandatory**
**Who should avoid**
- Broken or inflamed skin
- Active eczema, rosacea flare
- Immediately after shaving or waxing

==Is chemical exfoliant in a lotion or cream OK? Or should I get it only in toners and body-wash. Because the lotion will stay on the body overnight?==
Leaving chemical exfoliants on skin overnight is actually **BETTER**
==Why leave-on is more effective than rinse-off:==
|Factor|Leave-on (lotion/cream)|Rinse-off (bodywash/toner)|
|---|---|---|
|**Contact time**|Hours — all night|30–60 seconds|
|**Penetration**|Deep, thorough|Surface level only|
|**Effectiveness**|Much higher|Minimal|
|**Concentration needed**|Lower (gentler)|Higher (to compensate for short time)|
|**Irritation risk**|Lower (lower concentration)|Can be higher (needs high % to work fast)|
==The science behind it:==
- Chemical exfoliants like lactic acid and urea need **time** to break down keratin bonds
- A bodywash with lactic acid that you rinse off in 60 seconds does **almost nothing**
- A lotion with the same ingredient left on overnight works **significantly better**
- This is why dermatologists almost always recommend **leave-on formulas** for KP specifically
==What concentrations are safe to leave on:==
|Ingredient|Safe leave-on %|Avoid leaving on if above|
|---|---|---|
|Lactic acid|5–12%|20%+ (professional grade)|
|Urea|10–25%|40%+ (prescription)|
|Salicylic acid|0.5–2%|3%+|
|Glycolic acid|5–10%|15%+|
