**Data governance** is the set of rules, processes, and responsibilities that ensure data in an organization is **accurate, secure, accessible, and used correctly**.
---
**Simple analogy:**  
Think of data like money in a company. You need:
- A bank (storage)
- Rules on who can withdraw (access control)
- An audit trail (who withdrew what and when)
- Accuracy checks (the balance is correct)
Data governance is the **banking system for your data**.
---
**Key pillars:**
|Pillar|What it means|
|---|---|
|Access Control|Who can read, write, or delete what data|
|Data Quality|Is the data accurate, complete, consistent|
|Data Lineage|Where did this data come from, how was it transformed|
|Auditing|Who accessed what data and when|
|Data Discovery|Can people find and understand what data exists|
|Data Privacy|Is sensitive / PII data protected and compliant|
|Data Ownership|Who is responsible for each data asset|
---
**Why it matters:**
- **Compliance** — regulations like GDPR, India's DPDP Act require you to know where personal data is and who can access it.
- **Trust** — analysts and business users need to trust the data they use for decisions.
- **Security** — prevent unauthorized access to sensitive data.
- **Efficiency** — people can find data without asking around.
---
**Data governance vs related terms:**
|Term|What it is|
|---|---|
|Data Governance|The rules and processes around data management|
|Data Catalog|A tool that implements discovery and metadata management|
|Data Quality|A subset of governance focused on accuracy and completeness|
|Unity Catalog|Databricks' implementation of data governance|
---
**In short:** Data governance answers — _who owns the data, who can access it, where did it come from, and can we trust it?_
