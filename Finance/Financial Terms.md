==What is a tenure?==
- **Tenure** means the **fixed length of time** for which an agreement, investment, or role lasts.
- It defines **when something starts and when it ends**.
- During the tenure, the agreed **rules, benefits, and obligations apply**.
**Core idea**
- Think of tenure as the **time-bound container** for any commitment.
==What is interest?==
- **Interest** is the **cost of using money over time**.
- If you **borrow money**, interest is what you **pay extra**.
- If you **lend or deposit money**, interest is what you **earn extra**.
**Core idea**
- Money today is worth more than money tomorrow.
- Interest compensates for:
	- **Time** (waiting)
	- **Risk** (chance of not getting money back)
	- **Opportunity cost** (what else money could earn)
**Two basic roles**
- **Borrower** → pays interest
- **Lender / investor** → earns interest
==What is principal amount?==
- **Principal** is the **original amount of money** involved in a financial transaction.
- It is the **base amount** on which interest is calculated.
- It **excludes interest, fees, and penalties**.
**One-line summary**
- **Principal = the starting amount of money in any interest-based deal.**
==Types of interests==
**1. Simple Interest**
- Calculated **only on principal**.
- Linear growth.
- Used in short-term loans.
- Formula: `SI = P × R × T`
**2. Compound Interest**
- Calculated on **principal + accumulated interest**.
- Exponential growth.
- Used in FDs, savings, investments.
**3. Fixed Interest**
- **Rate stays constant** for entire tenure.
- Predictable returns.
- Common in FDs, some bonds.
**4. Floating (Variable) Interest**
- **Rate changes** with a benchmark.
- Can increase or decrease over time.
- Used in home loans, repo-linked loans.
**5. Reducing Balance Interest**
- Interest calculated on **outstanding amount**.
- Interest reduces as principal is repaid.
- Standard for modern loans.
**6. Flat Interest**
- Interest calculated on **full principal** for entire tenure.
- Looks cheap, but actually expensive.
- Seen in some personal loans.
**7. Nominal Interest**
- Stated rate, **not adjusted for inflation**.
**8. Real Interest**
- Nominal interest **minus inflation**.
- Shows true purchasing power gain.
**9. Penal Interest**
- Extra interest charged for **late payment or default**.
**10. Zero Interest (No-cost EMI)**
- Interest cost hidden via fees or discounts.
- Common in consumer financing.
**One-line takeaway**
- **Interest differs by how it’s calculated, whether it changes, and what it’s meant to achieve.**

==What is equity?==
- **Equity** means **ownership** in something.
- In finance, equity usually means **ownership in a company**.
**Equity in companies**
- When you buy a **share (stock)**, you buy **equity** in that company.
- You become a **part-owner**, proportional to shares held.
**What equity gives you**
- **Claim on profits** (dividends, if declared).
- **Claim on assets** if company is liquidated (after debt).
- **Voting rights** in major decisions (usually).
**How equity makes or loses money**
- **Price appreciation**: share price goes up.
- **Dividends**: company shares profits.
- **Loss**: share price can fall; no guaranteed returns.
**Equity vs debt**
- **Equity**:
	- Owner
	- Higher risk
	- Higher potential return
	- No fixed income
- **Debt**:
	- Lender
	- Lower risk
	- Fixed interest
	- Priority repayment
**Equity as an investment**
- **Direct equity**: buying shares yourself.
- **Equity mutual funds**: fund buys shares on your behalf.
- Long-term horizon recommended (5+ years).
**Simple example**
- Company worth ₹100 crore issues shares.
- You own 1% → your equity value = ₹1 crore (at that valuation).
**One-line summary**
- **Equity = ownership with risk and reward tied to business performance.**

==What is liquidation?==
- **Liquidation** is the process of **closing a business** and **selling its assets**.
- The money raised is used to **pay debts in a fixed legal order**.
- After all dues are paid, any remaining amount goes to **owners (equity holders)**.
**When liquidation happens**
- Company is insolvent (can’t pay debts).
- Business is shut down permanently.
- Court / regulator orders winding up.
**What gets liquidated**
- Cash, property, machinery.
- Inventory, investments.
- Intellectual property (brands, patents).
**Payment priority (simplified)**
1. Secured creditors (banks with collateral)
2. Unsecured creditors
3. Government dues
4. Equity shareholders (last)
