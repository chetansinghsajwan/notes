Antiandrogens are drugs that **block or reduce the effects of androgens** (male sex hormones like testosterone and DHT) in the body.
---
### Key Facts
- Don't always lower testosterone levels — some just **block its effects** at the receptor level
- Essential in MTF HRT to suppress masculinizing effects
- Used alongside estrogen for feminization
- Different types work through **different mechanisms**
---
### How They Work (Mechanisms)
|Mechanism|What it does|Examples|
|---|---|---|
|**Androgen Receptor Blocker**|Blocks testosterone from binding to receptors|Bicalutamide, Spironolactone|
|**Testosterone Suppressor**|Lowers testosterone production directly|GnRH Agonists, CPA|
|**5-alpha Reductase Inhibitor**|Blocks conversion of T to DHT (potent form)|Finasteride, Dutasteride|
|**Progestogenic**|Suppresses T via progestin activity|Cyproterone Acetate (CPA)|
---

---
### India-Specific Notes 🇮🇳
- **CPA is the most commonly prescribed** antiandrogen in India for MTF HRT
- CPA is **not available in the USA** — makes India's access relatively better
- Spironolactone is widely available as it's also used for blood pressure and acne
- GnRH agonists are available but rarely used due to **very high cost**
---

> In simple terms: **Antiandrogens are testosterone blockers — they stop your body from responding to or producing male hormones, allowing estrogen to feminize the body effectively.**
