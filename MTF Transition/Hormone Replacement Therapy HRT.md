# Timeline
### ==**1–3 months**==
- Skin becomes softer, less oily.
- Libido (sexual desires) may reduce.
- Erections become weaker.
### ==**3–6 months**==
- Breast buds start forming.
- Body fat starts shifting to hips/thighs.
- Emotional changes increase.
- Muscle mass starts reducing.
### ==**6–12 months**==
- Noticeable breast growth.
- Body hair growth slows.
- Testicular volume decreases.
- Spontaneous erections reduce further.
### ==**1–2 years**==
- Breast development continues (usually A–B cup).
- Final fat redistribution.
- Skin becomes thinner and more delicate.
- Hair thinning or baldness slowdown (depends on genetics).
### ==**2–3 years**==
- Maximum breast development plateau.
- Muscle strength stabilizes at lower levels.
- Emotional patterns stabilize.
# Voice Effect
Here’s the blunt truth in short points:
- MTF HRT (estrogen + anti-androgen) – Does NOT change the voice. – Vocal cords already thickened from testosterone during puberty; estrogen can’t reverse that. – Pitch, resonance, and depth usually stay the same. – Only voice training or voice feminization surgery can change it.
- FTM HRT (testosterone) – Yes, it changes the voice. – Testosterone thickens the vocal folds, dropping pitch. – Voice deepens within 3–12 months, stabilizes around 1–2 years.
- Stopping HRT – MTF: Voice stays the same as before (HRT never changed it). – FTM: Deepening is permanent; stopping T won’t reverse it.
Sources:• UCSF Transgender Care Guidelines• WPATH Standards of Care v8• University of California Voice & Swallowing Center
# Old Age Effect
Here’s the tight, to-the-point version you prefer:  
Effects of continuing HRT (MTF) in older age  
1. Breast tissue stays– Estrogen maintains breast volume and fat distribution.– Stopping later in life can reduce some fat but not glandular tissue.Source: Endocrine Society Clinical Practice Guideline 2017.  
  
2. Bone health protection– Continuing estrogen helps prevent osteoporosis.– Stopping HRT after decades increases fracture risk.Source: Endocrine Society; WPATH SOC8.  
  
3. Cardiovascular risk stays similar to cis women on HRT– Risk depends on dose, route, smoking, BP, cholesterol.– Transdermal estrogen (patch/gel) is preferred in older age due to lower blood-clot risk.Source: WPATH SOC8; UCSF Transgender Care.  
  
4. Blood clot (VTE) risk slightly increases with age– Highest with oral ethinyl estradiol (rarely used now).– Modern estradiol patches have very low added risk.Source: UCSF Transgender Care Guidelines.  
  
5. Skin & fat changes maintained– Softer skin, reduced body hair, and fat redistribution remain as long as estrogen is present.– Ageing still happens; collagen loss is natural.  
  
6. Sexual function changes stay stable– Lower libido & erectile function persist with estrogen + antiandrogen.– Stopping estrogen may raise testosterone again unless testes removed.  
  
7. Cancer risks– Breast cancer risk slightly increases but remains lower than in cis women.– Prostate cancer risk decreases but does not go to zero.Source: BMJ 2021 transgender cohort study; WPATH SOC8.  
  
8. Liver load is minimal with patches– Oral estrogen stresses the liver more; patches avoid first-pass metabolism.  
  
9. Routine monitoring becomes more important– Yearly: BP, lipids, HbA1c, prolactin, estradiol/testosterone.– Bone density scans every few years after 50+.
