# Antiandrogens Oral vs Injectables

> Note: The question asks about antiandrogens, but the research heavily covers **estradiol routes** when comparing oral vs injectable. I'll cover both angles clearly.
---
### Oral Antiandrogens
- Taken daily as tablets
- Most common forms: CPA, Spironolactone, Bicalutamide
- Convenient but rely on **consistent daily dosing**
- Spironolactone is described as a relatively weak antiandrogen
- Oral estradiol combined with oral antiandrogens fails to adequately reach testosterone suppression in up to one-third of patients
---
### Injectable Antiandrogens (GnRH Agonists)
- Most powerful testosterone suppressors available
- Injectable estradiol even as monotherapy was effective at testosterone suppression in 82.6% of patients, comparable with combination therapy with an antiandrogen
- GnRH agonists suppress testosterone to near-castrate levels
---
### Direct Comparison
|Factor|Oral Antiandrogens|Injectable (GnRH Agonists)|
|---|---|---|
|**Effectiveness**|Moderate–High|Very High|
|**T Suppression**|Partial to full|Near-complete|
|**Consistency**|Depends on daily compliance|Stable (monthly/quarterly)|
|**Liver impact**|Yes (especially CPA)|No|
|**Side effects**|More varied|Bone density loss (long-term)|
|**Cost (INR/month)**|₹100–₹800|₹3,000–₹15,000|
|**India availability**|✅ Easy|✅ Available but expensive|
---
### Key Takeaway
- Progestogen use was independently associated with lower testosterone concentration, whereas spironolactone had no significant effect on testosterone levels.
- For most people in India, **oral CPA or Bicalutamide** is the practical choice — effective and affordable
- Injectable GnRH agonists are reserved for cases where oral options aren't working or aren't tolerated
